## Design Context

### Users

Forge serves anyone working with PCB/CAD parts and SPICE models, from an absolute beginner making a first component to analogue and aerospace staff engineers validating production work. Beginners need plain-language guidance and safe defaults; experienced engineers need speed, exact values, provenance, and the ability to inspect or correct what Forge read.

### Brand Personality

Precise, trustworthy, and approachable. Forge should feel like a calm engineering instrument: rigorous enough for high-consequence work, but never intimidating or needlessly academic.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the interaction flow and functional structure established in `ui/forge-suite`. Refine its industrial, utilitarian visual language rather than redesigning the workflow. Ember is the restrained action/work-in-progress accent; cool blue communicates verified or locked information. Use warm-tinted neutrals, strong hierarchy, accessible contrast, and purpose-built light and dark themes. Avoid generic dashboard cards, decorative gradients, excessive glow, and visual effects that imply certainty the evidence does not support.

### Design Principles

1. Use progressive disclosure so a beginner can succeed without slowing an expert down.
2. Keep provenance visible: show what came from the datasheet, where it came from, and what was inferred, corrected, or refused.
3. Put the current decision, source, and next action on the same screen whenever practical.
4. Preserve the established intent-before-reading and package-before-CAD flow.
5. Prefer an honest refusal or explicit uncertainty over a plausible-looking unsupported result.
6. Treat accessibility, keyboard operation, responsive behavior, and readable technical data as core product behavior.
